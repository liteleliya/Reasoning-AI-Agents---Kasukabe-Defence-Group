“Our project studies how a team of language-model agents can make disagreement visible and test whether a counterfactual replay helps them recover. 
The agents share an append-only blackboard. Each message contains a prediction, an explanation, a dialogue tag, and a link to the message being answered.”

0:20–0:48 — System and replay

“When the scheduler detects an eligible disagreement, the system can compare ordinary continuation, forward rethink, and replay. 
Replay creates temporary branches to test a proposed replacement against the original message. The live history is preserved, and adoption is kept separate from correctness.”

[Open Explore runs. Select a case with a recorded replay, click First challenge, then step to the replay event and its inspector.]

0:48–1:22 — Paired baseline

“In our frozen paired baseline, 24 cases were run with six seeds: 144 shared prefixes and 432 arm outcomes. 
Fifty-two prefixes opened checkpoints. Standard continuation and replay each produced one automatically correct recovery; forward rethink produced none. 
Replay used more tokens, so this baseline does not show a replay advantage.”

[Click Results. Point to the checkpoint, recovery, adoption, and cost figures. Explain that adoption is not a correctness result.]

1:22–1:55 — Density and MedAgentBench

“We then tested counterfactual-agent density. The adaptive-policy run completed 1,680 of 2,000 sessions; its zero, one, and two-agent conditions each have 500 cases, while the three-agent condition is partial. The static-policy run completed 1,000 sessions. We compare the two policies on 250 shared cases at the three complete levels. Separately, our 30-task MedAgentBench baseline scored zero correct; 28 tasks stopped on invalid tool-action format.”

[On Results, scroll to MSCoRe density study, then MedAgentBench baseline. Point out the partial adaptive condition and the action-format failures.]

1:55–2:20 — Interpretation

“The density runs show that replay actions occurred, but they do not establish better answers. The adaptive policy rewards lower disagreement, not correctness. MedAgentBench exposed a tool-format problem that should be fixed before scaling. These are useful engineering and evaluation findings, not claims of general agent reliability.”

[Point to the combined interpretation and evidence-limit notes on Results.]

2:20–2:30 — Close

“Our contribution is an inspectable local system, reproducible experiment artifacts, and clear evidence about what still needs improvement.”

[End on the project title and repository link in the README.]
